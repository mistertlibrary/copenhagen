Useful Once Observed

The day after a test; the day before a break; planned sub days; lab cleanup days; that one Friday when nothing goes to plan.

Until someone observes them, those periods sit in a superposition of usefulness and uselessness. Lemme collapse that state vector. You bring the science; I'll bring literacy and research skills.

A Quantum of Assistance

I

Observation

5 minutes

A strange true story from the history of science, projector- and printer-ready. Each ends on a question your students can answer only if we teach a full period together.

II

Experiment

one period

We co-teach. I'm (a) Bohr: I run the case and the sources. You're Heisenberg: you keep the science honest (and serve as a bastion of grander wisdom). Students use your course content to test a claim against the evidence.

III

Report

several days

A research unit built on your course content alongside library databases, NoodleTools, and APA citations. You convey the scientific facts; I'll handle the research process and build materials.

How It Works

  • Your room or mine? I'll come to you or you can bring your class to the library, where there's room for stations and real books that students just might want to read.
  • Planning an absence? Hit Frontline, then talk to me.
  • You handle the science, while I, a prima donna, run the show (and perhaps share the stage). Everything comes readymade with nothing to grade (unless you want to grade it).

Why It Counts

  • New Jersey's administrative code calls for information literacy delivered by school library media specialists, alone or in partnership with classroom teachers.
  • Every sample is built from NJSLS and NGSS standards. The chemistry sample models radioactive decay (HS-PS1-8) and practices a core science skill: evaluating the validity and reliability of claims.
