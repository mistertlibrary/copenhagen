Certified Radioactive


Or,
The Superhero Comics Lied to Me?

In the 1920s, a New Jersey company sold a bottled water called Radithor. Each label promised the consumer a refreshing swig of “Certified Radioactive Water.” Every Radithor bottle held at least one microcurie each of two radium isotopes.

This was an actual product that people actually bought. But why would anyone drink it?

  1. Radium sits in group 2 of the periodic table. Which group 2 element does your body need most? Where does your body store it?
  2. Radium-226 decays by emitting an alpha particle. Write the nuclear equation.
  3. Radium-228 has a half-life of 5.75 years. A bottle bought in 1928 held 1 microcurie of it. How much remained in 1951? Radium-226 has a half-life of 1,600 years. About what fraction of it remained?
  4. One source (opens in a new tab) says Eben Byers drank 1,400 bottles. Another says he averaged three a day for about two years. Do the sources agree? How might you check?

Answers

  1. Calcium; bones and teeth.
  2. Ra-226 (88 protons) becomes Rn-222 (86 protons) plus He-4 (an alpha particle).
  3. 23 years is four half-lives: 1/16 of a microcurie, about 0.06. About 99 percent of the radium-226 remained.
  4. Not even close! Three a day for two years is about 2,190 bottles. Who counted? How? Where did each source find its number?

Ebenezer “Eben” McBurney Byers, a socialite, Pittsburgh steel executive and former U.S. Amateur golf champion, began drinking Radithor in 1927 on a doctor’s advice. (He had broken his arm in a fall and was trying to manage chronic pain.) Radium, like calcium, settled in his bones. He lost weight, then teeth, then most of his jaw; he died in 1932. The Federal Trade Commission had already ordered Radithor’s maker to stop calling it harmless, much less “A cure for the living dead,” “Perpetual Sunshine,” and a remedy for impotence (among other maladies).

Radium also glows and was used as a paint ingredient. What for? Where? When? Why? And who ultimately paid the price?

Teacher notes
  • Standards: HS-PS1-8 (nuclear processes); group trends on the periodic table. Question 4 establishes a research habit: since the sources disagree, we ask how each number was made.
  • Radium-228 decays by beta emission to actinium-228; radium-226 decays by alpha emission to radon-222.
  • Bailey Radium Laboratories was based in East Orange, New Jersey. William Bailey, its founder, was a Harvard dropout.
